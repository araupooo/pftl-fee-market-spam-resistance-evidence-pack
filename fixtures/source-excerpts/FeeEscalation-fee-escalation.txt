1: # Fees
2: 
3: Rippled's fee mechanism consists of several interrelated processes:
4: 
5: 1. [Rapid Fee escalation](#fee-escalation)
6: 2. [The Transaction Queue](#transaction-queue)
7: 
8: ## Fee Escalation
9: 
10: The guiding principal of fee escalation is that when things are going
11: smoothly, fees stay low, but as soon as high levels of traffic appear
12: on the network, fees will grow quickly to extreme levels. This should
13: dissuade malicious users from abusing the system, while giving
14: legitimate users the ability to pay a higher fee to get high-priority
15: transactions into the open ledger, even during unfavorable conditions.
16: 
17: How fees escalate:
18: 
19: 1. There is a base [fee level](#fee-level) of 256,
20:    which is the minimum that a typical transaction
21:    is required to pay. For a [reference
22:    transaction](#reference-transaction), that corresponds to the
23:    network base fee, which is currently 10 drops.
24: 2. However, there is a limit on the number of transactions that
25:    can get into an open ledger for that base fee level. The limit
26:    will vary based on the [health](#consensus-health) of the
27:    consensus process, but will be at least [5](#other-constants).
28: 
29: - If consensus stays [healthy](#consensus-health), the limit will
30:   be the max of the number of transactions in the validated ledger
31:   plus [20%](#other-constants) or the current limit until it gets
32:   to [50](#other-constants), at which point, the limit will be the
33:   largest number of transactions plus [20%](#other-constants)
34:   in the last [20](#other-constants) validated ledgers which had
35:   more than [50](#other-constants) transactions. Any time the limit
36:   decreases (i.e. a large ledger is no longer recent), the limit will
37:   decrease to the new largest value by 10% each time the ledger has
38:   more than 50 transactions.
39: - If consensus does not stay [healthy](#consensus-health),
40:   the limit will clamp down to the smaller of the number of
41:   transactions in the validated ledger minus [50%](#other-constants)
42:   or the previous limit minus [50%](#other-constants).
43: - The intended effect of these mechanisms is to allow as many base fee
44:   level transactions to get into the ledger as possible while the
45:   network is [healthy](#consensus-health), but to respond quickly to
46:   any condition that makes it [unhealthy](#consensus-health), including,
47:   but not limited to, malicious attacks.
48: 
49: 3. Once there are more transactions in the open ledger than indicated
50:    by the limit, the required fee level jumps drastically.
51: 
52: - The formula is `( lastLedgerMedianFeeLevel *
53: TransactionsInOpenLedger^2 / limit^2 )`,
54:   and returns a [fee level](#fee-level).
55: 
56: 4. That may still be pretty small, but as more transactions get
57:    into the ledger, the fee level increases exponentially.
58: 
59: - For example, if the limit is 6, and the median fee is minimal,
60:   and assuming all [reference transactions](#reference-transaction),
61:   the 8th transaction only requires a [level](#fee-level) of about 174,000
62:   or about 6800 drops,
63:   but the 20th transaction requires a [level](#fee-level) of about
64:   1,283,000 or about 50,000 drops.
65: 
66: 5. Finally, as each ledger closes, the median fee level of that ledger is
67:    computed and used as `lastLedgerMedianFeeLevel` (with a
68:    [minimum value of 128,000](#other-constants))
69:    in the fee escalation formula for the next open ledger.
70: 
71: - Continuing the example above, if ledger consensus completes with
72:   only those 20 transactions, and all of those transactions paid the
73:   minimum required fee at each step, the limit will be adjusted from
74:   6 to 24, and the `lastLedgerMedianFeeLevel` will be about 322,000,
75:   which is 12,600 drops for a
76:   [reference transaction](#reference-transaction).
77: - This will only require 10 drops for the first 25 transactions,
78:   but the 26th transaction will require a level of about 349,150
79:   or about 13,649 drops.
80: 
81: - This example assumes a cold-start scenario, with a single, possibly
82:   malicious, user willing to pay arbitrary amounts to get transactions
83:   into the open ledger. It ignores the effects of the [Transaction
84:   Queue](#transaction-queue). Any lower fee level transactions submitted
85:   by other users at the same time as this user's transactions will go into
86:   the transaction queue, and will have the first opportunity to be applied
87:   to the _next_ open ledger. The next section describes how that works in
88:   more detail.
