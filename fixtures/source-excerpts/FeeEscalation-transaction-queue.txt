90: ## Transaction Queue
91: 
92: An integral part of making fee escalation work for users of the network
93: is the transaction queue. The queue allows legitimate transactions to be
94: considered by the network for future ledgers if the escalated open
95: ledger fee gets too high. This allows users to submit low priority
96: transactions with a low fee, and wait for high fees to drop. It also
97: allows legitimate users to continue submitting transactions during high
98: traffic periods, and give those transactions a much better chance to
99: succeed.
100: 
101: 1. If an incoming transaction meets both the base [fee
102:    level](#fee-level) and the [load fee](#load-fee) minimum, but does not have a high
103:    enough [fee level](#fee-level) to immediately go into the open ledger,
104:    it is instead put into the queue and broadcast to peers. Each peer will
105:    then make an independent decision about whether to put the transaction
106:    into its open ledger or the queue. In principle, peers with identical
107:    open ledgers will come to identical decisions. Any discrepancies will be
108:    resolved as usual during consensus.
109: 2. When consensus completes, the open ledger limit is adjusted, and
110:    the required [fee level](#fee-level) drops back to the base
111:    [fee level](#fee-level). Before the ledger is made available to
112:    external transactions, transactions are applied from the queue to the
113:    ledger from highest [fee level](#fee-level) to lowest. These transactions
114:    count against the open ledger limit, so the required [fee level](#fee-level)
115:    may start rising during this process.
116: 3. Once the queue is empty, or the required [fee level](#fee-level)
117:    rises too high for the remaining transactions in the queue, the ledger
118:    is opened up for normal transaction processing.
119: 4. A transaction in the queue can stay there indefinitely in principle,
120:    but in practice, either
121: 
122: - it will eventually get applied to the ledger,
123: - it will attempt to apply to the ledger and fail,
124: - it will attempt to apply to the ledger and retry [10
125:   times](#other-constants),
126: - its last ledger sequence number will expire,
127: - the user will replace it by submitting another transaction with the same
128:   sequence number and at least a [25% higher fee](#other-constants), or
129: - it will get dropped when the queue fills up with more valuable transactions.
130:   The size limit is computed dynamically, and can hold transactions for
131:   the next [20 ledgers](#other-constants) (restricted to a minimum of
132:   [2000 transactions](#other-constants)). The lower the transaction's
133:   fee, the more likely that it will get dropped if the network is busy.
134: 
135: If a transaction is submitted for an account with one or more transactions
136: already in the queue, and a sequence number that is sequential with the other
137: transactions in the queue for that account, it will be considered
138: for the queue if it meets these additional criteria:
139: 
140: - the account has fewer than [10](#other-constants) transactions
141:   already in the queue.
142: - all other queued transactions for that account, in the case where
143:   they spend the maximum possible XRP, leave enough XRP balance to pay
144:   the fee,
145: - the total fees for the other queued transactions are less than both
146:   the network's minimum reserve and the account's XRP balance, and
147: - none of the prior queued transactions affect the ability of subsequent
148:   transactions to claim a fee.
149: 
150: Currently, there is an additional restriction that the queue cannot work with
151: transactions using the `sfPreviousTxnID` or `sfAccountTxnID` fields.
152: `sfPreviousTxnID` is deprecated and shouldn't be used anyway. Future
153: development will make the queue aware of `sfAccountTxnID` mechanisms.
